# sipx 1.0.0-rc.19

Seven stories close. Five of them were implemented concurrently in isolated worktrees and merged as
one wave, and the most valuable thing in this candidate is what only appeared when they met. A
prerelease on the same terms as its predecessors: stable promotion still requires use by an
application nobody in this repository wrote.

## Install

```sh
cargo add sipx-call@=1.0.0-rc.19
cargo install --locked --version '=1.0.0-rc.19' sipx-cli
```

## What the wave found

**A checker had been reading part of every file.** `check-audio-claims.py` cut each source at the
*first* `#[cfg(test)]` anywhere in it. `dsp/graph.rs` declares one on a test-only constructor at line
964 of 2,552, so `Stage`, `Buffers`, `Live`, `Slot` and `SlotRef` — the entirety of a chain an
earlier story had found *by hand* — were invisible to **every rule in the checker**, not merely to
the one being widened. 24 such attributes across 12 files in 5 crates. The cut is now anchored at
column 0, which is what its docstring always claimed.

That matters more than the immediate fix. This repository's redaction rules are enforced rather than
reviewed, and an enforcement that silently skips part of the tree reports clean on it. Widening the
reader found three further types rendering call audio in a `Debug` record, two written this week.
The reader now follows a public `Debug` through up to six private hops — six because it was measured:
reports saturate at four, the population at six, and seven selects nothing more.

**And then it caught the next thing.** One story added a new public module while another was fixing
the blindness. At its own merge base that module was invisible, so its checks passed honestly; on the
merged tree the newly-sighted checker refused it. Its types now carry the rationales the rules ask
for — the octets in question are fuzzer-generated indices and three bytes of deliberate garbage, so
rendering them is what makes a minimised crash reproducible.

Two more defects existed only in the combination: a test that asserted the *wording* of a message
another story reworded, and an intra-doc link to a type that does not exist, which fails the docs
build under `-D warnings` and cannot be seen by any check narrower than building the whole API
reference.

## Fixed

**A peer could declare a frame ceiling of four billion samples.** The supervised worker protocol
accepted a `Hello` whose `max_samples` was anything at all, so a runtime sized its buffers from a
number the far side chose. Now refused above the contract's maximum, with the effective ceiling
bounded whatever the caller passes. Found by a hand-written case *before* the campaign that guards
it: 1.2 million executions, a 40-seed corpus replayed byte for byte and held against modification,
and a CI job at the other fuzz targets' budget.

**Byte buffers outside the relay path no longer print themselves.** Six carriers across the crates
where a call comes to rest render a length rather than their octets. One consequence was not asked
for and is kept: bounding every field rather than only the buffers removes a verbatim `Authorization`
header from any record-level diagnostic.

**A dialled leg that goes away is reported.** An application was told a leg had been established and
never told it ended, so the snapshot the contract calls authoritative listed it for the rest of the
call. The ending carries its own three-word cause rather than reusing the five that describe a call
ending, because two of those would have been unreachable *and* meaningless here — which is the same
defect this story fixes, one level down at the value instead of the row.

## Added

**One DSP stage can step out of a live chain and back in**, without replacing the chain or opening a
new epoch. Two refusals are part of the feature rather than caveats on it: a bypass the *runtime*
imposed under the miss budget cannot be undone by an application, or a failing stage could be pinned
on the media path by restoring it after every failure; and a supervised stage cannot be bypassed at
all, because its output lags its input by the declared deadline and the gap would be paid for in
audio from the wrong part of the call.

**`peers --timeout`.** The wait for a registrar's first NOTIFY was bounded in a previous candidate,
and the bound was a constant no operator could move — while the acceptance row claiming it was
operator-controllable had been ticked. It is now true. `0` returns to the event client's Timer N.
Resolution is funded from the attempt like every other command, which retires the last exception in
the rule that every published deadline states what it covers.

## Changed

**The queue is ordered.** Every open story carries a unique priority; dependencies rank above what
consumes them, and work needing a resource this machine does not have ranks below work that can start
now. Eight duplicate priorities and ten stories with none had made "the top ready story" a question
the board could not answer.

## Known and not fixed

The fuzzing harness for the worker protocol is a public module of a published crate. Its author's
reason is sound — the oracle must drive the real crate-private callers — but the workspace's own
convention puts harnesses in the test crate, and the better arrangement splits the vocabulary out and
leaves a single opaque entry point behind. Recorded rather than rushed.
