# Mute and unmute a call's outbound audio

## Goal
A local media gate: stop contributing audio to the far end without renegotiating the session and
without touching reception — distinct from hold, which is a signalled state the far end sees.

## Acceptance
- [ ] Muting a call suppresses its outbound audio at the media layer; unmuting restores it. No
      re-INVITE is sent, the SDP direction is unchanged, and the far end's hold state is
      unaffected — the difference from `reinvite(Direction::SendOnly)` is stated in the docs.
- [ ] Whether mute sends silence frames or stops sending RTP is decided in the design and
      recorded, with the RTCP consequence stated (RFC 3550 §6 — the stream's reports must remain
      truthful either way), and the chosen behaviour is tested from the receiving side.
- [ ] Reception is untouched: a muted call still receives audio and DTMF, and `recv_digit`,
      recording and quality statistics keep working while muted.
- [ ] Mute state is queryable and transitions are observable as events (`C-3`).
- [ ] Failing-first test: `a_muted_call_contributes_no_audio`.

## Progress
- Implemented. `MediaSession::set_muted`/`is_muted` and the `gated` frame mapping in
  `crates/sipx-media/src/session.rs`; `Call::mute`/`unmute`/`is_muted` and
  `CallEvent::Muted`/`Unmuted` in `crates/sipx-call`. Tests: five media-layer unit tests in
  `session.rs` and seven integration tests in `crates/sipx-call/tests/mute.rs`, all asserting the
  outbound half **from the receiving side**.
- **The decision the story left open: mute substitutes silence, it does not stop the stream.**
  Recorded in `docs/designs/app-sdk.md` and in the `session.rs` module docs. A muted session sends
  exactly the packets it would have sent unmuted — same pacing, sequence numbers and timestamps —
  with the audio replaced by encoded silence. Suppressing the packets was rejected because it
  closes the NAT pinhole (media-inactivity teardown behind an SBC), leaves the far end's jitter
  buffer to restart on unmute so the first word after it is clipped, and makes "muted"
  indistinguishable on the wire from "the far end has gone away".
- **The RFC 3550 §6 consequence fixes where the gate goes, not just what it does.** A sender
  report's packet and octet counts (§6.4.1) describe what actually went on the wire, and the far
  end computes loss from sequence numbers — so the gate sits *before the packet is built*. A mute
  placed one step later (build the packet, drop the datagram) would overstate this side's own
  reports *and* manufacture apparent loss at the far end out of a caller who was merely quiet.
  Asserted from the receiving side: the far end's `packets_received` equals our `packets_sent` and
  its `cumulative_lost` stays zero across a mute.
- **DTMF is not gated, in either direction.** A telephone event is generated by this endpoint on
  purpose — the way a keypad tone is on a handset — not picked up by a microphone, so a muted
  caller can still answer an IVR. `Frame::Dtmf` passes through the gate untouched, and there is a
  test for it at both layers.
- **A relayed payload (`Frame::Encoded`, a bridge) is replaced by this session's own silence**
  rather than forwarded. Its bytes are opaque — whatever the other leg negotiated — and nothing in
  the send loop can silence them in place.
- **Mute survives a media restart.** `move_media_if_changed` carries the flag onto the replacement
  session: without it a re-INVITE that moves the media — the far end changing address or codec,
  which this side did not ask for and cannot refuse — would unmute the call behind the
  application's back.
- Events are emitted on the *transition* only, using the previous value returned by
  `set_muted`, so muting an already-muted call reports nothing and two callers cannot race
  read-then-write. The contract has no wire event for this by design (`app-contract.md` §5.3);
  what a remote app sees is `media.muted` on the next snapshot (§5.2), which these transitions let
  the interpreter build from a push instead of by polling.
- No registry change: mute changes no RFC claim. `rfc-report.py --check` agrees.

## Notes
- `mute` does not exist anywhere in the workspace today; the closest primitives are
  `Direction::Inactive`/`SendOnly` re-INVITEs (signalled, visible to the peer) or simply not
  calling `send`/`play` (which no host holding a bridged call can arrange).
- Needed by the host (`crates/sipx-app`): `mute` and `unmute` are contract verbs.
